### **EU Digital Mission – Governance, Legal & Ethical Compliance Framework**

---

## **1. Purpose**

This document defines how every digital service — from parish websites to blockchain donation ledgers — must remain compliant with:

* **EU Regulations**

  * *General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR, 2016/679)*
  * *Digital Services Act (DSA, 2022/2065)*
  * *ePrivacy Directive*
  * *NIS2 Cybersecurity Directive*

* **Canonical (Catholic Church) Governance**

  * Transparency in stewardship and finances
  * Ethical communication and digital conduct
  * Preservation of spiritual dignity and non-commercial purity

* **Ethical AI & Transparency Standards**

  * EU AI Act (anticipated compliance clauses)
  * Principles of explainability, consent, and accountability

---

## **2. Compliance Philosophy**

> “Compliance is not an obstacle — it’s the cathedral’s foundation.”

The architecture assumes **compliance-by-design**.
This means compliance is not a feature added later, but a **permanent structural layer** built into:

1. **Code** – API endpoints enforce consent and privacy rules.
2. **Infrastructure** – servers obey data residency and encryption standards.
3. **Documentation** – every deployment is auditable.
4. **AI Behavior** – models respect data rights and ethical governance.

## **4. Data Protection & GDPR Compliance**

### 4.1 Data Categories

| Type                | Examples                                | Storage                        | Retention              |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ---------------------- |
| Personal            | Names, emails, phone numbers            | PostgreSQL                     | Until account deletion |
| Sensitive           | Religious affiliation, donation history | Encrypted PostgreSQL (AES-256) | 5 years max            |
| Logs                | Access, performance, error traces       | MongoDB (Immutable)            | 180 days               |
| Blockchain Metadata | Wallet hash, donation ID                | Ethereum (pseudonymous)        | Permanent              |

### 4.2 Consent Management

* Explicit consent forms for all data collection.
* Consent tokens stored in PostgreSQL with timestamp and purpose.
* Users can revoke consent via `/api/gdpr/revoke` endpoint.

### 4.3 Right to Erasure (“Forget Me”)

* Django GDPR module enables one-click deletion workflow.
* Associated blockchain records anonymized via irreversible hash unlinking.
* Backups automatically purge after 72h grace period.

### 4.4 Data Minimization

* Collect only what’s necessary for service delivery.
* No third-party ad or tracking cookies permitted.
* AI training data anonymized before any processing.

### 4.5 Data Residency

* Country-specific PostgreSQL nodes retain localized data.
* Google Cloud region pinned to `europe-west1` or `europe-north1`.
* Cross-border transfer allowed only via EU Standard Contractual Clauses.

## **5. Digital Services Act (DSA) Compliance**

### 5.1 Transparency Obligations

* Each site publishes an **Impressum** (legal identity statement).
* Automated AI-generated content labeled as “AI Assisted”.
* Monthly transparency report auto-generated by system cron job.

### 5.2 Content Moderation

* CMS enforces role-based publishing rights (no anonymous posting).
* User reports auto-log to `moderation_audit` collection.
* Appeals workflow integrated via Bitrix24 task chain.

### 5.3 Advertising & Commercial Integrity

* No programmatic advertising allowed.
* Donations and event promotions must be nonprofit-aligned.
* Cookie banners limited to essential, functional cookies only.

| -------------------- | ------------------------------ | --------- |

## **7. Canonical Governance Compliance**

### 7.1 Financial Transparency

* All donation ledgers (on-chain + off-chain) must be readable by diocesan auditors.
* Monthly donation reports auto-exported as PDF & CSV.
* Smart contract source verified on Etherscan.

### 7.2 Ethical Content

* No political, commercial, or non-pastoral messaging.
* Mandatory human review for AI-generated homilies or spiritual text.
* All AI systems must disclose authorship clearly: “Assisted by AI Architect”.

### 7.3 Hierarchical Oversight

| Role                        | Responsibility                     |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Parish Admin                | Local content & data handling      |
| Diocese Officer             | Regional financial reporting       |
| Central Canonical Oversight | Compliance review & audit trail    |
| Vatican Liaison             | Ethical AI governance coordination |

### 7.4 Spiritual Integrity Clause

> “The mission’s technology shall never distort, exploit, or replace human spiritual guidance, only amplify the Church’s authentic voice.”

## **8. AI Ethics & Accountability**

### 8.1 Transparency

* All chatbots display AI origin notice and link to privacy policy.
* All generated content includes metadata tag `ai_source=true`.

### 8.2 Data Boundaries

* No reuse of user conversation data for model retraining without explicit consent.
* LLMs operate on sanitized copies only.

### 8.3 Bias & Accuracy

* LLMs tested against EU religious sensitivity corpus.
* Automated moderation flags theological inaccuracies for human review.

### 8.4 Explainability

Every AI decision (e.g., content filtering, risk scoring) must include a **trace log** stored in `/ai_decisions/` with:

```
decision_id
timestamp
input_summary
reasoning_summary
output_action
review_status
```

## **11. Incident Response Workflow**

1. **Detection:** Sentry / OpenTelemetry anomaly
2. **Classification:** Security or Data breach
3. **Containment:** Suspend affected microservice
4. **Notification:**

   * Internal → Bitrix24 incident task
   * External → DPA (72-hour GDPR window)
5. **Recovery:** Restore from verified backup
6. **Postmortem:** Publish non-sensitive summary
